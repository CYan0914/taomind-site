不是不想做，是一想到整件事有多大，就瘫住了。It's not that you don't want to. It's that the size of the whole thing freezes you.

其安易持，其未兆易谋，其脆易泮，其微易散。为之于未有，治之于未乱。合抱之木，生于毫末；九层之台，起于累土；千里之行，始于足下。为者败之，执者失之。是以圣人无为，故无败；无执，故无失。民之从事，常于几成而败之。慎终如始，则无败事。是以圣人欲不欲，不贵难得之货。学不学，复众人之所过。以辅万物之自然，而不敢为。What is at rest is easy to hold; what has not yet shown a sign is easy to plan; what is brittle is easy to break; what is small is easy to scatter. Act on it before it exists; govern it before it is disordered. A tree that fills the embrace grows from a sprout; a nine-story terrace rises from a heap of earth; a journey of a thousand miles begins beneath one's feet. One who acts on it destroys it; one who grasps it loses it. Therefore the sage does not act, and so does not fail; does not grasp, and so does not lose. In people's handling of affairs, they always fail when they are close to success. If one is as careful at the end as at the beginning, one has no ruined affairs. Therefore the sage desires without desire, and does not prize rare goods. He learns without learning, and returns to what the multitude has passed by. Thus he helps the ten thousand things be natural, and does not dare to act.——《道德经》第六十四章— Tao Te Ching, Chapter 64

老子怎么说What Lao Tzu says

第六十四章说千里之行，始于足下。这句话被用滥了，但前面还有更狠的一句：九层之台，起于累土。老子没说要有决心，他说的是从最小的一捧土开始。让你瘫住的不是事情本身，是你脑子里的整件事。Chapter 64 says a journey of a thousand miles starts under your feet. The line gets quoted to death, but earlier in the same chapter: a nine-story tower begins with a basket of earth. Lao Tzu doesn't ask for resolve. He asks for the smallest possible amount of dirt. What freezes you isn't the task. It's the whole thing in your head.
